# Clausemap — Brand Guide

*Working title: Memory Palace · IBM Bob 2.0 hackathon · 27 Sep 2026*
*Built from the concept brief in `bob/memory-palace-requirements.md`. Assumptions, since there was no time to ask: the feel is precise, sober and trustworthy (from the brief); you want name ideas; the brand has to work on the web app, the cover image, the slides and the video today.*

---

## The idea

An AI agent that turns a long contract or report into a map of **who owes what to whom, by when**, where every node and link opens the exact passage it came from, checked word for word against the page. Built for analysts and reviewers in due diligence, contract review, compliance and consulting.

---

## Mission & values

**Mission:** give every analyst the whole shape of a document in a minute, and a verified line of source behind every item on it.

- **Verbatim or nothing.** If a quote isn't on the page, it isn't on the map.
- **Structure before detail.** Show the shape first, so the reading goes where it matters.
- **Show what we dropped.** Say what couldn't be verified instead of hiding it.
- **Documents stay theirs.** Nothing stored, nothing trained on, and the provider named.
- **A map, not a verdict.** We show what the document says; the analyst decides what it means.

---

## Name ideas

Why not "Memory Palace": a memory palace is a technique for *memorizing* things. Your buyers don't want to remember a contract, they want to *verify* it. The name promises the wrong job and sounds like a study app. MEMORA, Mindscape and DocVerse from the pasted plan lean playful for a buyer whose first question is confidentiality.

I can't run trademark or domain searches from here, so check any name before committing (a quick web search and a domain lookup take five minutes).

**Descriptive: clear in one second, which matters for judges**
- **Clausemap** *(lead)*. Says both halves of the product: contracts, and a map. Easy to say and spell in English, Dutch and German. Downside: it undersells reports, which is fine while contracts lead the demo.
- **Termscape.** Terms plus landscape; a little softer, a little vaguer.

**Metaphor: surveying, which is where your GIS background gives the brand a real story**
- **Trigpoint.** A trig point is a fixed, surveyed marker every measurement is taken from, like a verified quote. Distinctive and ownable-feeling, but it needs a sentence of explanation. Strong long-term brand.
- **Cadastre.** The official map of who owns which parcel, which is exactly "who owes what". Precise and serious, but hard to say for many people.

**Evidence-first**
- **Verbatim.** Names the differentiator directly. But it's a common word and already a well-known storage-media brand, so it's hard to own.
- **Quoted.** Short and honest, but generic.

**Recommendation:** **Clausemap** for today, because judges and buyers understand it instantly. Keep **Trigpoint** in mind if you grow beyond contracts.

## Brand personality

**Archetype:** the Sage (precise, evidence-led) with a touch of the Explorer: a surveyor who charts unfamiliar ground carefully and marks every point.

**Traits**
- **Exact:** numbers, pages and quotes, never approximations.
- **Calm:** no hype, no alarm, even when a contract has problems.
- **Candid:** says what it couldn't verify.
- **Quietly capable:** the product does impressive work and doesn't announce it.

**The vibe:** a well-drawn survey map on good paper. Clean lines, clear labels, a legend that tells you exactly what you're looking at, and a small orange stake wherever something was measured.

---

## Voice & tone

**We sound like** a careful senior analyst briefing a colleague. **We never sound like** a hype-driven AI launch.

| We say | Not |
|---|---|
| We say what we found and where. | "Unlock the hidden insights in your documents!" |
| We give numbers and pages. | "Lots of key obligations were detected." |
| We state limits plainly. | "Our advanced AI ensures complete accuracy." |
| We use the reader's words: parties, obligations, deadlines. | "Entities, nodes and semantic relationships." |

**Example lines**
- **Product line:** "Clausemap reads a contract and maps who owes what to whom, by when. Click anything to see the exact line on the page."
- **Dropped items:** "3 items left off the map: their quotes weren't found on the cited page."
- **Scanned PDF:** "This looks like a scan, so there's no text to read. Text-based PDFs only for now."
- **Notice:** "A map of what this document says, not legal advice. Check every item against the source."
- **Demo voice-over:** "NotebookLM maps topics. Clausemap maps obligations, and every line on it is quoted verbatim."

## Tagline / motto

1. **"See the whole deal. Cite every line."** *(lead)*. The two halves of the product in six words: overview, then proof. Works as the cover-image line.
2. "Who owes what, to whom, by when. Sourced."
3. "The map is the overview. The quote is the proof."
4. "Every obligation on the map. Every quote on the page."

<span style="display:inline-block;width:16px;height:16px;background:#5B6570;border-radius:3px;vertical-align:middle;"></span> **Slate** `#5B6570` — secondary text: page numbers, captions, metadata.

<span style="display:inline-block;width:16px;height:16px;background:#2A7A74;border-radius:3px;vertical-align:middle;"></span> **Date** `#2A7A74`
<span style="display:inline-block;width:16px;height:16px;background:#9A6B12;border-radius:3px;vertical-align:middle;"></span> **Amount** `#9A6B12`
<span style="display:inline-block;width:16px;height:16px;background:#6E7378;border-radius:3px;vertical-align:middle;"></span> **Topic** `#6E7378`

**Usage: 60 / 30 / 10.** About 60% Chart Paper, 30% Survey Ink and Slate, 10% Stake Orange. The map itself adds the node colors; keep orange rare everywhere else so it still means "look here".

**Contrast check (WCAG)**
- Survey Ink on Chart Paper: 13.0:1. Use for all body text.
- Slate on Chart Paper: 5.4:1. Fine for small secondary text.
- White on Stake Orange: 4.6:1. Passes AA for button labels.
- Stake Orange on Chart Paper: 4.1:1. Large text and icons only, not small body text.
- Survey Ink on Highlighter: 11.2:1.
- All node colors on Chart Paper: 4.2:1 or better, above the 3:1 needed for graphics.
- Contour: 1.9:1. Lines and dividers only.

For dark mode or video title cards, invert: Chart Paper text on Survey Ink (13.0:1), with Stake Orange as the accent.

## Typography

Three faces, each with one job. The key device: **anything quoted from the document is set in a serif; everything the product says is in sans.** Users learn in seconds which words are the document's and which are ours.

- **Headings and UI: IBM Plex Sans** (SemiBold for headings, Regular for UI). Engineered, sober, excellent at small sizes. It's also IBM's own open-source typeface, a quiet nod to the event that doesn't borrow IBM's brand. Free: [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/IBM+Plex+Sans)
- **Quotes from the document: Source Serif 4** (Regular, Italic). Reads like a printed contract, clearly different from the UI. Free: [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Source+Serif+4)
- **Data: IBM Plex Mono** for page numbers, amounts, dates and counts. Aligns in columns and signals "exact value". Free: [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/IBM+Plex+Mono)

**In use**
- Heading (Plex Sans SemiBold, 28 px): **Master Services Agreement — 14 parties and obligations mapped**
- Node panel label (Plex Sans Regular, 14 px): Supplier *must deliver* Acceptance Report
- Quote (Source Serif 4, 16 px, on Highlighter): *"The Supplier shall deliver the Acceptance Report no later than thirty (30) days after the Effective Date."*
- Source line (Plex Mono, 12 px, Slate): `p. 7 · verified`

## Logo direction

A starting direction for today's cover image and favicon, not finished artwork.

**Direction 1: Trig mark (lead).** A combination mark: a small solid triangle in Stake Orange (a survey trig point) sitting on three short horizontal Survey Ink lines, like lines of text, with **clausemap** in lowercase IBM Plex Sans SemiBold beside it. The triangle is a measured point on a page: verified evidence on a document. Works at favicon size as the triangle and lines alone. Fastest to make: two shapes and a word.

**Direction 2: Linked points.** A wordmark: **clausemap** in Plex Sans SemiBold, with two small dots under the "c" and the "p" joined by a thin line, one dot in Survey Ink and one in Stake Orange. Two parties, one obligation between them. Subtle and clean; weaker at favicon size.

**Direction 3: Folded corner.** A lettermark: a lowercase "c" inside a page outline with a folded top-right corner, with a single orange dot on the fold. Clear "document" signal, but closer to generic file icons.

**Avoid:** brains, lightbulbs, palaces or temples (the old name), glowing neural-network webs, gavels and scales of justice, magnifying glasses, and 3D or gradient effects. They all read as generic AI or generic legal.

**Cover image for today:** Chart Paper background; a small, clean map of 8 to 12 nodes in the node colors on the left; the Trig mark and the tagline "See the whole deal. Cite every line." in Survey Ink on the right; one highlighted quote in Source Serif under the tagline.

## Putting it together

Clausemap sells to people whose job is to be right and to prove it, so the brand borrows from the most trusted kind of map there is: a survey on paper. Ink and paper carry the structure, the orange stake marks what's been measured, and the highlighter and serif make every quote unmistakably the document's own words. The voice is a careful colleague who gives page numbers and admits what it couldn't verify. Every choice points back to the one promise the product makes: see the whole deal, and cite every line.
